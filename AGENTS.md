# AGENTS.md

## SEO-Playbook

SEO-Playbook is an encyclopedic SEO knowledge base. Its job is to build, connect, evaluate, and maintain reliable knowledge about search engine optimization—from fundamentals to advanced search systems and experimental approaches.

This repository is a knowledge base, not a generic SEO checklist or a collection of shortcuts. Every document should help readers understand what a concept is, how it works, what evidence supports it, where it applies, and where its limits begin.

## Project Identity and Architecture

The 17 top-level knowledge domains documented in `README.md` are the agreed project architecture. Preserve them unless the project owner explicitly approves a structural change.

Use `README.md` as the public reference for the project's purpose, architecture, scope, and documentation principles.

## k41 Identity and Editorial Voice

The universal identity source for k41 is [k41-core](https://github.com/K41-Origin/k41-core). The project must respect that identity while adapting its expression to the subject and purpose of SEO-Playbook.

Write **k41** in lowercase. Never stylize it as **K41**.

The k41 voice is:
- Natural, casual, and fluent in internet culture.
- Confident and expressive, with a clear point of view.
- Skeptical and critical; claims should be questioned rather than accepted because they are popular.
- Sharp and occasionally sarcastic or satirical when the context genuinely calls for it.
- Characterful rather than generic, stiff, or needlessly academic.

Criticism must have reasoning behind it. Do not replace arguments with insults, force slang into every paragraph, or perform edginess for its own sake.

### Editorial priorities

The k41 character is a voice, not a license to compromise the work. Apply priorities in this order:

1. Truth, factual accuracy, and intellectual honesty.
2. Evidence, sound reasoning, and technical precision.
3. Clarity, usefulness, and relevance to the reader's intent.
4. A distinctive k41 voice.

Confidence must match the strength of the evidence. Clearly distinguish facts, interpretations, observations, opinions, assumptions, hypotheses, and unknowns. State uncertainty when it matters.

Use Indonesian as the default language unless the document's context or audience calls for another language. Keep established English SEO and technical terms where translating them would reduce precision. Prefer natural, readable sentences over bureaucratic phrasing.

## SEO and Search Quality

SEO-Playbook documents how search systems and SEO concepts work; it must not invent certainty about systems that are partly opaque.

For claims about Google Search, prioritize Google's official documentation and statements. For technical mechanisms and broader search research, use relevant primary research, standards, patents, and credible independent sources as appropriate.

Never imply that a tactic is a confirmed ranking factor merely because it correlates with rankings, appears in an anecdote, or is widely repeated. Separate:
- What an official source explicitly confirms.
- What research or reproducible testing demonstrates.
- What practitioners observe.
- What the author infers.
- What remains a hypothesis or unknown.

Do not frame SEO as manipulation for its own sake. Explain search intent, user value, technical constraints, trade-offs, and policy implications.

## Evidence and Source Standards

Use this source hierarchy as a guide, not as a substitute for evaluating the actual quality and relevance of each source:

- **P1 — Official primary sources:** Search engine documentation, official policies, product documentation, standards bodies, and direct statements from responsible organizations.
- **P2 — Primary technical and research sources:** Peer-reviewed papers, original experiments, patents, specifications, and technical publications.
- **P3 — Independent corroboration:** Credible reporting, expert analysis, and well-supported independent studies.
- **P4 — Practitioner observations:** Case studies, field tests, and experience reports with methods and limitations disclosed.
- **P5 — Hypotheses and opinions:** Plausible explanations or proposals that do not yet have adequate validation.

Cite sources close to the claims they support. Prefer original sources over pages that merely repeat them. Record publication or access dates when freshness matters. Do not fabricate citations, quotations, test results, or source details.

## Domain 17 — k41-version

**k41-version — SEO Hacks, Tricks & Experimental Strategies** documents unconventional approaches, experiments, automation, competitive intelligence, growth experiments, and original k41 discoveries.

The domain is not a dumping ground for magic tricks or unsupported ranking-factor claims. Every entry should explain, where relevant:
- The problem, objective, and context.
- The proposed mechanism and assumptions.
- The method or implementation.
- Test conditions, observations, and results.
- Evidence quality and reproducibility.
- Risks, limitations, and Google Search policy implications.
- What would confirm, weaken, or falsify the claim.

Use the following maturity labels consistently:

- **Verified:** Supported by sufficiently strong and relevant evidence.
- **Tested:** Tested under documented conditions; results may not generalize.
- **Experimental:** Being investigated; conclusions remain provisional.
- **Hypothesis:** Proposed but not adequately tested.
- **Unverified:** Evidence is currently insufficient.
- **Deprecated:** No longer recommended or relevant under the documented conditions.

A label describes the maturity of the specific claim or technique, not a permanent guarantee. Reassess it when new evidence appears.

## Documentation Workflow

Before adding or materially revising a topic:

1. Identify its correct domain and relationship to existing topics.
2. Define the question, scope, and terminology.
3. Research the strongest available sources.
4. Separate established knowledge from interpretation and uncertainty.
5. Explain mechanisms and practical implications without overstating them.
6. Document limitations, risks, and relevant policy considerations.
7. Add references and cross-links where useful.
8. Review accuracy, clarity, structure, and the k41 editorial voice.

Adapt the structure to the topic. A useful document may include definition, context, mechanisms, components, examples, related concepts, evidence, practical implications, limitations, and references. Do not force irrelevant sections into every page.

## Repository Governance

- Keep the 17-domain architecture consistent with `README.md`.
- Avoid duplicate pages; improve or cross-link existing knowledge where appropriate.
- Use descriptive filenames and headings.
- Keep claims traceable to sources or clearly label them as interpretation or hypothesis.
- Do not silently convert experimental observations into general rules.
- Update outdated material when evidence, systems, or policies change.
- Keep changes focused and explain meaningful structural changes.
- Do not claim that a topic has been comprehensively researched when it has not.

The goal is not to sound like an SEO textbook written by committee. The goal is to build a knowledge base that is sharp, useful, technically credible, and honest about what it knows—and what it doesn't.
