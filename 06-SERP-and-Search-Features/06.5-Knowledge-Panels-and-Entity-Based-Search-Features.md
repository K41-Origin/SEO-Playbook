# 06.5 — Knowledge Panels & Entity-Based Search Features

**Domain:** [06 — SERP & Search Features](README.md)  
**Topic ID:** 06.5  
**Status:** Documented and source-checked  
**Review date:** 2026-11-19

Knowledge panels are entity-oriented information displays. They organize facts and links around a person, place, organization, work, or another identifiable thing—not around one publisher page answering one query. In Google's documented implementation, panels are generated automatically from its understanding of entities and information gathered from multiple sources. They are not a profile card that a website owner can simply publish into Search. [1][2]

This topic covers the visible presentation, source relationship, entity distinctions, and publisher-facing limitations of knowledge panels. It does not teach semantic entity modeling, local SEO execution, or the internal construction of a search engine's knowledge graph.

## 1. What a Knowledge Panel Is

Google describes a knowledge panel as an information box that may appear when a user searches for an entity represented in its Knowledge Graph. Its purpose is to provide a concise snapshot of information about that subject and help users explore it further. [1]

An **entity** is the identifiable subject the information is about: for example, a particular person, organization, place, creative work, or product. An entity is not merely a string of text. Two people can share a name; one organization can have several name variations; and one word can refer to unrelated things. Entity-oriented presentation depends on the system associating information with the intended subject rather than treating every matching string as the same thing.

Google's Knowledge Graph is described as a database of facts about people, places, and things. Google says those facts are assembled from multiple sources, including public sources, licensed data, and information supplied by content owners through supported processes. [2]

The important distinction: **the Knowledge Graph is an underlying information resource; a knowledge panel is one possible visible Search presentation of information associated with it.** They are related, but they are not interchangeable terms.

Google's documentation is the evidence base for the examples in this topic. Do not assume every search engine uses the same Knowledge Graph model, panel taxonomy, source policy, or publisher controls.

## 2. Knowledge Panels and Entity-Oriented Presentation

Knowledge panels are designed around a subject, not around a single URL. Depending on the entity and query, a panel may combine information such as:

- The entity's name or title and a short identifying description.
- A longer description or summary.
- One or more images.
- Key facts relevant to the entity type.
- Links to an official website or relevant profiles.
- Subject-specific information, such as creative works, episodes, or team rosters. [3]

This is a descriptive component model, not a fixed template. Google explicitly notes that panel contents vary by topic. A panel for a person will not necessarily contain the same fields as one for a place, organization, or entertainment property. [3]

The desktop layout historically placed many panels alongside the main result list. Google has also documented that Knowledge Graph information on mobile may appear as multiple panels interspersed among results rather than as one side column. Treat that as a documented explanation of responsive presentation, not a promise about every current mobile layout. [3]

A panel may also appear, disappear, or change composition as the query and Search context change. Its presence should be recorded as an observation tied to a query and context—not treated as a permanent property of an entity. [2]

## 3. Knowledge Panels, Featured Snippets, and Other Similar Displays

Several SERP features can show concise information prominently. Their visual similarity does not make them the same feature.

| Presentation | Primary organizing unit | What distinguishes it | Canonical topic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge panel | An entity | Aggregates facts and references about an identifiable subject. | 06.5 |
| Featured snippet | A source page and selected passage | Elevates an excerpt from a publisher page to answer a particular query. | 06.4 |
| Standard organic result | A URL | Presents a page as a conventional search result with title link, attribution, and snippet. | 06.2 |
| Structured-data rich result | An eligible page/result | Adds a supported enhancement to an organic result under feature-specific rules. | 06.3 |
| Business Profile | A local business | A business-focused Google presentation for a business serving customers at a location or service area. | 06.6 for visible local presentation; Domain 13 for local strategy |
| AI-generated answer | A generated response | Presents synthesized output with its own source and interaction model. | 06.11 |

This table is a practical distinction model for the playbook, not an official Google taxonomy. Google explicitly distinguishes knowledge panels from Business Profiles, and describes featured snippets as selected page excerpts. [1][2][4]

A featured snippet may appear next to Knowledge Graph information, but the excerpt and the entity panel remain separate presentations. One is centered on a passage from a source page; the other is centered on an entity. [4]

## 4. Information Sources and Attribution

Google says information in knowledge panels is collected from multiple sources across the web. Depending on the subject, those sources may include public web content, open datasets, licensed data partners, and information provided by content owners through supported channels. Google also says that images can come from different sources; some may be selected by verified representatives, while other image collections may be sourced automatically from Google Images results. [1][3]

This has several implications:

1. **A panel is not a one-source citation.** Different fields may be supported by different sources.
2. **A linked source is not necessarily the source of every fact.** Do not assume that the official website link supplied every visible field.
3. **A panel summary is not a complete biography or definitive record.** It is a search presentation assembled for a particular context.
4. **A source being used does not mean every statement on that source is adopted.** The public documentation does not expose a complete field-by-field provenance map or the internal confidence calculations.

When auditing a panel, record the visible source links and compare important facts against reliable primary sources. Do not infer hidden source weights, entity confidence scores, or internal graph relationships from the panel alone.

## 5. How Google Generates and Displays Knowledge Panels

Google states that knowledge panels are automatically generated. Its systems determine when, where, and how a panel is relevant to a user's query; Google does not manually create or delete panels on request. The panel's information can change as information on the web changes and as Google processes feedback. [1][2][4]

What the public documentation supports:

- Panels are generated automatically rather than published as ordinary website pages.
- A panel may be shown when Google's systems determine that entity information is relevant to the query.
- Panel content can draw on multiple information sources.
- Panel availability and composition can change.
- Feedback and verified suggestions can be considered as part of correction processes. [1][2][4]

What the documentation does **not** disclose:

- The complete entity-resolution or knowledge-graph construction pipeline.
- A universal threshold for when an entity receives a panel.
- A guaranteed list of source types or source priorities for every field.
- A fixed scoring formula for panel eligibility or visibility.
- A reliable timetable for panel creation, modification, or disappearance.
- A publisher-controlled command that guarantees a panel.

The table below is an explanatory model for reading the public behavior, not Google's internal pipeline:

1. Information about a subject exists across sources.
2. Google's systems associate available information with an entity.
3. Search determines whether entity-oriented information is useful for a particular query and context.
4. Search may render a knowledge panel or another Knowledge Graph-based presentation.
5. Information and presentation may change as sources, systems, and feedback change.

Do not present this model as a disclosed sequence of technical stages. The internal mechanisms remain only partially public.

## 6. Claiming, Verification, and Suggesting Corrections

Google provides a verification process for eligible people or official representatives who want to suggest changes to a knowledge panel. Claiming a panel is not the same as creating one, owning the underlying Knowledge Graph record, or gaining unrestricted editorial control. Not all panels are claimable. [1][5]

Google's documented process allows a verified representative to suggest changes to supported information. Suggestions are reviewed; Google may accept or reject them based on available evidence and policy. Google also says some content, such as descriptions, is sourced from elsewhere and cannot simply be replaced with a custom publisher-written paragraph. Correcting the originating source may be the appropriate route. [5]

Practical distinctions:

- **Claiming/verification:** establishes that a person is an eligible subject or representative who can submit verified suggestions.
- **Suggesting an edit:** asks Google to review a proposed correction or update; it is not an automatic publication command.
- **General feedback:** allows users to report an issue even when they are not the verified representative.
- **Source correction:** may be necessary when a panel field reflects inaccurate information on an upstream source.
- **Removal request:** follows Google's applicable legal or policy processes; it is not a routine content-management control. [1][2][5]

Use primary, publicly accessible evidence when submitting a correction. A preference is not proof that a fact is wrong, and verification does not guarantee that every requested change will be accepted.

## 7. Knowledge Panels Versus Google Business Profiles

Google states that Business Profiles can look similar to knowledge panels, but they are specifically for businesses that serve customers at a physical location or within a designated service area. Google directs those businesses to Business Profile rather than the general knowledge-panel claim process. [1][2][5]

This distinction matters because the workflows and ownership differ:

- **Knowledge panels** are entity-oriented displays generated from Google's Knowledge Graph and related information sources.
- **Business Profiles** are a Google product for eligible local businesses to manage their business presence on Google Search and Maps.
- A business-related entity panel and a local Business Profile may look similar in parts of the interface, but they are not interchangeable products.

Domain 06.5 documents the entity-panel presentation. Domain 06.6 owns local and map-based result presentation. Domain 13 owns local SEO strategy and execution. Do not turn this page into a Business Profile setup guide.

## 8. Structured Data, Entities, and Common SEO Misreadings

Structured data can describe entities and relationships in machine-readable form, but the existence of markup does not establish that Google will create or display a knowledge panel. Google's knowledge-panel documentation describes automatic generation from information available across sources; it does not document a special markup type or publisher switch that guarantees a panel. [1][2]

Keep these concepts separate:

- **Entity identification:** determining which real-world subject a name or reference denotes.
- **Knowledge graph:** a structured representation of entities and facts/relationships.
- **Structured data:** markup that expresses information about page content in a machine-readable format.
- **Knowledge panel:** a user-visible Search presentation that may draw on Knowledge Graph information.
- **Semantic SEO:** the broader strategy and implementation of entity-aware content and semantic relationships, owned by Domain 09.

Common misreadings:

- **"Add Organization schema and Google will create a knowledge panel."** Not supported. Structured data is not a documented guarantee of panel creation or display. [1][2]
- **"A knowledge panel is just a rich result."** No. A panel is entity-oriented; a rich result is an enhancement to an eligible search result under separate feature rules. See 06.3.
- **"Claiming a panel means you control it."** No. Verification enables eligible representatives to suggest changes; Google retains review and display decisions. [5]
- **"Every fact in a panel comes from the official website."** No. Google describes multiple sources and data partners. [1][3]
- **"If a panel disappears, the entity has been removed from the Knowledge Graph."** Not established. Google says panel display depends on query relevance and can change automatically. Panel visibility alone does not reveal the state of every underlying record. [2]
- **"Wikipedia is the only source that matters."** Google identifies Wikipedia as one commonly cited source among many, not the sole source. [3]
- **"Knowledge panels and local business profiles are the same thing."** Google explicitly distinguishes them. [1][2][5]

## 9. Observing and Auditing Knowledge Panels

When documenting a panel, preserve enough context to make the observation interpretable:

1. Search engine and product surface.
2. Exact query and the entity the query is intended to identify.
3. Market/location, language, device, and observation date.
4. Whether the panel appeared, and where it appeared in the SERP.
5. Visible fields: title, description, image, facts, profiles, and links.
6. The source URLs visibly linked from the panel.
7. Any ambiguity or entity-mixing issue, such as information about two similarly named subjects appearing together.
8. Whether the observation was repeated under a different device or query context.

For important factual claims, compare panel content with primary sources and note any discrepancies. Do not treat a screenshot as evidence of Google's internal graph structure, source weights, or permanent panel eligibility.

## Key Takeaways

- A knowledge panel is an entity-oriented Search presentation, not a result card for one specific URL.
- Google's Knowledge Graph is an underlying information resource; a knowledge panel is one possible visible presentation of information associated with it.
- Google says panels are automatically generated and can draw on multiple public, licensed, and owner-provided sources.
- Panel contents, layout, and visibility can vary by entity, query, device, and context.
- Eligible verified representatives can suggest corrections, but they do not gain unrestricted control over panel content or display.
- Business Profiles are distinct from general knowledge panels.
- Structured data does not guarantee that Google will create or display a knowledge panel.
- A panel is not proof that every displayed fact is complete, current, or correct; verify important claims against reliable sources.
