# 06.11 — AI-Powered SERP Features & Search Experiences

[← Domain 06 — SERP & Search Features](README.md)

## Scope

This topic documents the **user-visible AI-powered search experience**: generated answer layouts, source links and attribution, conversational follow-ups, transitions between search modes, and interactive generative elements where officially documented.

The scope is presentation and interaction—not a tour of model architecture, a speculative ranking-factor list, or a playbook for gaming AI citations. Domain 02 owns operational query processing, retrieval orchestration, response assembly, and serving. Domain 03 owns ranking systems and candidate evaluation. Domain 16 owns the broader evolution and strategic implications of AI-driven discovery.

The examples and product behavior below are Google-specific unless explicitly stated otherwise. Do not generalize them to every search engine or AI search product.

## 1. AI-Powered Search as a SERP Experience

Traditional SERPs primarily present ranked links and specialized result modules. AI-powered search adds generated responses that synthesize information and provide links for further exploration.

These experiences can coexist with conventional results. Their presence, layout, depth, and interaction options depend on the product, query, market, language, device, account context, and ongoing product changes. A generated answer is not a replacement taxonomy for every other SERP feature.

Google describes AI Overviews as an AI-generated snapshot intended to help users understand a topic and explore supporting websites. They are shown when Google's systems determine that the feature adds value beyond the conventional results; they do not appear for every query. [1][2]

Google describes AI Mode as a more extensive AI search experience for nuanced questions, exploration, reasoning, and comparisons. Users can ask follow-up questions and explore supporting web links. [2][3]

These are distinct experiences with overlapping capabilities—not interchangeable labels:

| Dimension | AI Overviews | AI Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Primary role | Generated overview within the Search results experience | Dedicated, deeper AI-powered search experience |
| Typical use | Get an initial synthesis and discover sources | Explore complex questions, comparisons, and follow-ups |
| Interaction | Read, open supporting links, and where available continue into AI Mode | Ask follow-up questions and continue a conversational search |
| Output | Generated summary with supporting links and, depending on the interface, additional interactive elements | More extensive generated response with links and additional interaction capabilities |
| Availability | Conditional; shown when Google's systems determine it is useful | Product and access availability can vary by market, language, device, and account |

This table is a practical comparison of documented product roles, not a promise that every interface will expose the same controls.

## 2. AI Overviews: Generated Summaries in Search

### 2.1 Visible presentation

An AI Overview is a generated response displayed within Google Search results. Depending on the query and current interface, it can include:

- A synthesized response or summary.
- Supporting website links and source references.
- Inline links embedded in response text.
- Expand/collapse or continuation controls.
- Related follow-up prompts or an entry point into AI Mode.
- Relevant visual or interactive elements where supported.

The exact layout is not fixed. Google has continued to change how links are displayed, including adding clearer inline links and more context around sources. The October 15, 2026 announcement describes updates to inline links, source context, timely articles, and other ways to discover websites from AI experiences. Treat that announcement as a dated product update, not a permanent UI specification. [4]

### 2.2 When an Overview appears

AI Overviews are conditional. Google's documentation says they are designed to appear when generative AI can add value beyond what users might already get from classic Search. Therefore:

- A query may show an AI Overview in one observation and not another.
- A complex-looking query does not guarantee an Overview.
- Absence of an Overview is not evidence that a topic is ineligible for AI features.
- A screenshot cannot establish a universal display rule.

Record the exact query, locale, language, device, date, and visible Search context when studying an Overview. [1][2]

### 2.3 AI Overview is not a featured snippet

Both can provide a prominent answer, but their presentation mechanisms differ:

- **Featured snippet (06.4):** a selected excerpt from a page, presented as an answer with a source link.
- **AI Overview (06.11):** a generated synthesis that may draw on multiple sources and present supporting links.

Do not call an AI Overview a featured snippet just because it appears near the top of the results page or answers the query. Likewise, it is not a rich result (06.3) or a Knowledge Panel (06.5). Similar screen real estate does not make the features equivalent.

## 3. AI Mode: Conversational Search

### 3.1 Core interaction model

AI Mode supports longer questions and iterative exploration. Users can ask a question, receive a generated response with web links, and continue with follow-up questions. Google documents a query fan-out technique in which a question is divided into subtopics and searched across multiple data sources; results are then brought together into a response. This is Google's high-level description of its product, not a complete disclosure of internal orchestration or retrieval architecture. [3]

The visible interaction can include:

- An initial question and generated response.
- Supporting website links.
- Follow-up questions that retain conversational context.
- New questions within the same conversation.
- Entry paths from Search or an AI Overview.
- Multimodal inputs such as images, voice, or other supported inputs, depending on the current product surface and availability.
- Interactive generated visuals such as diagrams, charts, or simulations where supported.

The user-facing experience belongs here. Internal query decomposition, retrieval, ranking, model orchestration, and response serving remain in Domains 02–04 and Domain 16 according to their canonical ownership.

### 3.2 Follow-up questions and conversational context

A follow-up question continues an existing exploration rather than necessarily starting a completely independent search. Google documents that users can ask follow-up questions in AI Mode and, on supported mobile experiences, move from an AI Overview into AI Mode while carrying over the original search context. [2][3]

For an observation, record:

1. The original query.
2. The exact follow-up question.
3. Whether the interface indicates that prior context is retained.
4. The visible response and supporting links after the follow-up.
5. Whether the interaction stays in the same AI surface or opens another Search experience.

Do not infer the full internal conversation-state mechanism from the visible continuity of the interface.

### 3.3 Multimodal and interactive experiences

AI Mode can support input and output beyond a plain text question. Google's current help documentation describes interactive visuals that can combine generated explanations with custom-coded diagrams, charts, and simulations, including controls such as sliders and toggles. The same documentation states that this interactive-visual capability is currently available to signed-in users in English. Availability is therefore conditional, not universal. [3]

Google has also documented visual and live camera interactions in AI Mode. Those capabilities overlap with visual search entry points, but the ownership boundary remains clear:

- Domain 06.8 owns Google Images, Lens, and image/video result presentation.
- Domain 06.11 owns multimodal interaction when it is part of an AI-powered conversational search experience.
- Domain 02 owns the internal processing and serving mechanisms.
- Domain 16 owns broader multimodal AI and search evolution.

Do not re-document Lens mechanics here. Describe only the AI-specific interaction and link to 06.8 for the visual-search surface.

## 4. Sources, Citations, and Links in Generated Responses

### 4.1 What supporting links do

AI-powered responses can expose links to web pages used as supporting sources or recommended destinations. These links help users verify claims, explore details, and reach original material. Their visual treatment can include inline links, source cards, grouped links, or other layouts that change over time. Google has explicitly announced updates intended to make links more visible and provide more context about sources. [1][4]

A source link inside a generated response is not identical to a standard organic result:

- A standard organic result has its own result title, URL, snippet, and presentation components (06.2).
- A featured snippet presents a selected page excerpt (06.4).
- An AI response may synthesize information and attach supporting links at specific points or in a separate source area.

The link's placement inside generated text does not by itself reveal the complete source-selection or ranking process.

### 4.2 Citation presence is not an endorsement or guarantee

A visible citation indicates that the product is associating a source with part of the response or offering it for further exploration. It does not establish that:

- Every sentence in the generated response is fully supported by that one source.
- The source is the only source considered.
- The generated answer accurately represents the source.
- The cited page is the definitive authority for the topic.
- A site will be cited whenever it is indexed or relevant.
- Citation position exposes a stable ranking weight.

Readers should open the sources, compare important claims across reliable references, and check primary material when accuracy matters. Google warns that AI Mode and AI Overviews can make mistakes or misinterpret context and recommends checking important information in more than one place. [2][3]

### 4.3 Inline links and source context

Inline links embedded in generated response text can make it easier to explore a specific concept without leaving the answer context. Google's October 2026 announcement describes increasing inline-link presence and adding more context around sources in AI Mode and AI Overviews. Because this is an evolving interface, record the actual link treatment and date observed rather than assuming every response will show the same pattern. [4]

Do not conflate source links with:

- **Sitelinks** in standard organic results (06.2).
- **Related searches** or **People Also Ask** interactions (06.10).
- **Knowledge Panel source attribution** (06.5).
- **Merchant or product links** in shopping modules (06.9).

Each has a different presentation context and canonical topic.

## 5. AI Overview and AI Mode as a Connected Journey

Google has documented a more seamless transition from an AI Overview into AI Mode. In the supported mobile experience, users can open the detailed Overview and submit a follow-up question; the original search context carries over into the conversational experience. Google also announced a broader mobile transition across regions and languages where both features are supported. Product availability and exact controls can still change. [2][5]

A useful journey model:

1. **Initial search:** the user submits a conventional Search query.
2. **Generated overview:** an AI Overview appears when Google's systems determine it is useful.
3. **Source exploration:** the user opens an inline link or supporting source.
4. **Deeper exploration:** the user continues into AI Mode, where supported.
5. **Follow-up:** the user asks a more specific or related question.
6. **Further verification:** the user checks linked sources and conventional Search results.

This is a conceptual interaction journey, not a mandatory sequence. Users may enter AI Mode directly, skip the Overview, open a source immediately, or return to ordinary results.

## 6. Reliability, Limitations, and User Controls

Generative responses can be incomplete, inaccurate, misleading, or missing context. Google explicitly warns that AI Overviews and AI Mode can make mistakes and recommends checking multiple sources, trying alternative phrasings, and submitting feedback when something appears wrong. [2][3]

For responsible use:

- Treat the generated response as a starting point, not the final authority.
- Open supporting links for claims that matter.
- Compare sources, especially for medical, legal, financial, safety, or time-sensitive topics.
- Check publication dates and the original context of quoted or summarized material.
- Distinguish a source's actual statement from the model's synthesis.
- Use the product's feedback controls when the answer appears incorrect.

Google documents a Web filter that can show text-based web links without AI Overviews and other Search features. This is a way to access a link-focused results view; it is not the same as disabling the underlying AI feature for a site or user. [2]

## 7. Website Eligibility and Search Appearance: What Can Be Said

This topic is about visible presentation, but a minimum eligibility explanation prevents a common misconception: AI visibility is not a separate magic markup category.

Google's Search Central documentation states that a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet to be eligible as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Google says there are no additional technical requirements or special AI-specific structured data required for inclusion. Meeting requirements does not guarantee crawling, indexing, serving, or citation. [1]

Google also describes AI features as using relevant links and, in some cases, query fan-out to explore related subtopics and sources. These are provider-level descriptions; they do not justify reverse-engineering a universal citation formula or claiming that a particular content format guarantees selection. [1]

The following boundaries apply:

- Domain 10 owns crawlability, indexability, robots directives, snippet controls, and technical implementation.
- Domain 08 owns content production, quality, and maintenance.
- Domain 03 owns ranking systems and candidate evaluation.
- Domain 02 owns retrieval and serving architecture.
- Domain 14 owns site-owner performance measurement.
- Domain 16 owns broader AI-search strategy and ecosystem evolution.

Google's current guidance says existing SEO fundamentals remain relevant and explicitly rejects the need for special AI files or special structured data solely to appear in these features. Do not turn generic advice such as “write for AI” into an unsupported technical requirement. [1]

## 8. Measurement and Reporting Boundary

Google states that traffic from AI Overviews and AI Mode is included in the overall Search Console Performance report under the **Web** search type. It is not documented as a separate search type in that guidance. [1]

This topic records the visible feature and its interaction. It does not define a complete analytics method, attribution model, or AI visibility KPI. Domain 14 owns performance measurement and interpretation. Avoid claiming that a change in clicks or impressions was caused by an AI feature without suitable evidence and a defensible measurement design.

## 9. Observation and Audit Framework

For a reproducible AI-SERP observation, capture:

1. Search engine and product surface.
2. Exact initial query and any follow-up questions.
3. Observation date and time.
4. Country/region and interface language.
5. Device, operating system, and browser or app.
6. Account/sign-in state and relevant product access conditions, where known.
7. Whether an AI Overview appeared, and where it appeared in the results.
8. Whether AI Mode was available and how the user entered it.
9. Visible answer structure: headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, visuals, or interactive controls.
10. Source-link format: inline links, source cards, grouped references, or other visible treatment.
11. Link destinations and the specific claims or passages they appear to support.
12. Follow-up behavior and whether prior context visibly carries over.
13. Any personalization or multimodal controls that were actually observed.
14. Errors, missing context, stale information, or unsupported statements found during source checking.
15. Whether the observation was repeated under a controlled comparison.

Keep observations separate from interpretation. A single screenshot does not establish a universal interface, a stable source-selection rule, or hidden ranking behavior.

## Key Takeaways

- AI Overviews and AI Mode are distinct but connected Google Search experiences.
- AI Overviews provide conditional generated summaries; AI Mode supports deeper conversational exploration.
- Supporting links help users explore and verify sources, but citation presence does not guarantee correctness, endorsement, or future inclusion.
- Generated responses can be wrong. Verify important claims against the linked sources and other reliable references.
- Google's published query fan-out explanation is a high-level product description, not a complete disclosure of internal architecture.
- Google says ordinary Search eligibility requirements apply; no special AI-only markup or files are required.
- Domain 06.11 owns what users see and how they interact. It does not own hidden retrieval/ranking architecture or the broader AI-search strategy.
