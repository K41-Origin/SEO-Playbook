# 09.10 — Semantic Model Validation, Maintenance & Governance

Model semantik bukan artefak yang dibuat sekali, dipajang di diagram, lalu dianggap selesai. Entitas berubah, definisi berkembang, sumber bukti direvisi, relasi bisa kedaluwarsa, dan struktur website ikut bergerak. Tanpa validasi dan tata kelola, model yang awalnya rapi pelan-pelan berubah jadi museum asumsi.

Domain 09.10 menetapkan cara memeriksa integritas model semantik, mengelola perubahan, menjaga provenance, dan memastikan representasi website tetap selaras dengan model yang disetujui. Fokusnya adalah **kualitas dan keberlanjutan model pengetahuan**, bukan audit editorial, audit teknis website, atau pengukuran performa SEO.

## 1. What Does Semantic Model Validation Mean?

**Semantic model validation** adalah proses sistematis untuk memeriksa apakah model semantik memenuhi definisi, batasan, aturan, bukti, dan tujuan yang telah ditetapkan untuk model tersebut.

Validasi tidak menjawab satu pertanyaan abstrak seperti “apakah model ini benar?”. Ia memecahnya menjadi pemeriksaan yang dapat dijelaskan:

- Apakah entitas diidentifikasi dan dibedakan secara konsisten?
- Apakah atribut dan identifier digunakan sesuai definisinya?
- Apakah relasi memiliki makna, arah, konteks, dan bukti yang tepat?
- Apakah schema, taxonomy, atau ontology digunakan secara konsisten?
- Apakah setiap assertion memiliki provenance dan status yang dapat ditelusuri?
- Apakah model tetap berada dalam scope yang disepakati?
- Apakah struktur website merepresentasikan model dan kebutuhan informasi tanpa mengubah maknanya?

Validasi harus selalu menyebutkan **objek yang diperiksa, kriteria, bukti, metode, hasil, dan batas pemeriksaan**. Label “validated” tanpa rincian itu cuma stiker.

### 1.1 Validation is not the same as truth

Model dapat lolos pemeriksaan struktur tetapi tetap memuat klaim faktual yang salah. Sebaliknya, satu assertion bisa benar berdasarkan bukti, tetapi belum memenuhi constraint teknis suatu representasi.

Pisahkan setidaknya empat pertanyaan:

| Dimension | What is checked | What a pass does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Structural conformance | Apakah data mengikuti schema, vocabulary, dan constraint yang ditetapkan? | Tidak membuktikan bahwa semua pernyataan faktual benar. |
| Logical consistency | Apakah kumpulan aksioma dan assertion saling bertentangan menurut semantik formal yang digunakan? | Tidak membuktikan bahwa model lengkap atau sesuai realitas. |
| Evidence and provenance | Apakah assertion memiliki sumber, konteks, waktu, dan dasar penerimaan yang dapat ditelusuri? | Tidak otomatis membuktikan bahwa sumbernya benar atau mutakhir. |
| Domain and architecture fitness | Apakah model masih sesuai scope, kebutuhan informasi, dan representasi website yang disetujui? | Tidak membuktikan performa SEO atau keberhasilan bisnis. |

Perbedaan ini penting. SHACL, misalnya, mendefinisikan cara memvalidasi RDF graph terhadap kondisi yang dinyatakan dalam shapes graph. Hasilnya adalah laporan validasi terhadap kondisi tersebut—bukan sertifikat bahwa setiap fakta di graph benar di dunia nyata. [1]

## 2. What Must Be Validated?

Gunakan dimensi berikut sebagai kerangka pemeriksaan. Kriteria konkretnya harus ditentukan berdasarkan jenis model, tujuan, risiko, dan kebutuhan pengguna.

### 2.1 Entity identity and boundaries

Periksa apakah:

- Satu entitas tidak terpecah menjadi beberapa record tanpa alasan identitas yang kuat.
- Dua entitas berbeda tidak digabung hanya karena memiliki label serupa.
- Identifier, preferred label, alias, dan deskripsi tidak saling tertukar.
- Entity boundary tetap jelas ketika cakupan model berkembang.
- Perubahan identitas atau penggabungan record memiliki bukti dan jejak keputusan.

Jangan menganggap label yang sama sebagai bukti identitas yang sama. Pemeriksaan ini meneruskan prinsip identitas dan disambiguasi di [09.3](09.3-Entity-Attributes-Identity-and-Disambiguation.md).

### 2.2 Attribute and relationship integrity

Periksa apakah:

- Atribut dipakai untuk jenis entitas yang sesuai.
- Nilai atribut memiliki tipe, unit, bahasa, atau konteks yang benar bila relevan.
- Predicate memiliki definisi yang stabil dan tidak digunakan sebagai istilah serba guna.
- Arah relasi tidak terbalik.
- Domain, range, cardinality, symmetry, transitivity, atau inverse behavior hanya diterapkan ketika memang didefinisikan.
- Relasi temporal memiliki konteks waktu yang memadai.
- Co-occurrence, similarity, atau association tidak dinaikkan menjadi relasi faktual tanpa bukti.

Definisi dan constraint harus dapat ditelusuri ke schema atau keputusan model yang relevan. Jangan mengandalkan intuisi editor sebagai satu-satunya validator.

### 2.3 Schema, taxonomy, and vocabulary consistency

Periksa apakah:

- Class, property, concept, dan label digunakan secara konsisten.
- Hierarki tidak memiliki siklus atau konflik yang dilarang oleh aturan model.
- Preferred labels dan alternative labels mengikuti kebijakan bahasa dan penamaan.
- Mapping antar-vocabulary tidak mengklaim equivalence yang lebih kuat daripada bukti.
- Perubahan definisi tidak diam-diam mengubah arti record yang sudah ada.

SKOS mendefinisikan sejumlah integrity conditions untuk konsistensi data modelnya, sekaligus menjelaskan bahwa pemeriksaan tersebut berfokus pada konsistensi terhadap model SKOS. Itu contoh konkret bahwa aturan validasi harus mengikuti semantik vocabulary yang dipakai, bukan daftar aturan generik yang diasumsikan berlaku untuk semua model. [2]

### 2.4 Provenance, evidence, and assertion status

Untuk assertion penting, simpan informasi yang cukup untuk menjawab:

- Siapa atau sistem apa yang membuat assertion?
- Sumber apa yang mendukungnya?
- Kapan sumber atau assertion diperoleh?
- Apakah assertion dikutip, diverifikasi, diinferensikan, disengketakan, atau belum terselesaikan?
- Siapa yang menyetujui perubahan status?
- Apa alasan assertion diterima, ditolak, atau diganti?

PROV-O menyediakan vocabulary untuk merepresentasikan entitas, aktivitas, dan agen yang terlibat dalam produksi suatu data atau artefak. Ia dapat menjadi referensi untuk provenance, tetapi tidak wajib dipakai oleh semua proyek dan tidak otomatis menilai kredibilitas sumber. [3]

### 2.5 Scope and model boundaries

Periksa apakah model:

- Masih menjawab tujuan yang dinyatakan.
- Tidak diam-diam menyerap konsep di luar domain.
- Tidak menghilangkan batasan, pengecualian, atau konteks yang mengubah interpretasi.
- Memisahkan fakta, asumsi, inferensi, dan unresolved questions.
- Tidak menambah kompleksitas tanpa kebutuhan yang jelas.

Scope validation berbeda dari topical coverage assessment. Domain 09.8 menilai kecukupan cakupan model terhadap scope dan kebutuhan informasi. Domain 09.10 memastikan scope itu tetap eksplisit, dipatuhi, dan perubahan batasnya dikelola.

### 2.6 Alignment between semantic model and website architecture

Periksa apakah:

- Page roles dan content groupings masih mewakili konsep yang dimaksud.
- Page boundaries tidak menggabungkan konsep yang harus dibedakan atau memisahkan satu kebutuhan informasi tanpa alasan.
- Label kategori tidak mengubah atau mengaburkan makna konsep.
- Relasi konseptual yang dipakai untuk mengatur informasi tidak disalahartikan sebagai klaim faktual atau kewajiban hyperlink.
- Setiap perubahan model yang berdampak pada arsitektur memiliki pemilik dan tindak lanjut.

Domain 09.9 menetapkan cara menerjemahkan model ke information architecture. Domain 09.10 memeriksa konsistensi dan mengelola perubahan hubungan antara keduanya.

## 3. Validation Methods: Choose the Right Tool for the Question

Tidak ada satu validator yang dapat memeriksa semua dimensi sekaligus. Pilih metode berdasarkan jenis klaim yang ingin diperiksa.

### 3.1 Manual and expert review

Gunakan review terstruktur untuk memeriksa:

- Ketepatan definisi dan entity boundaries.
- Apakah predicate mencerminkan makna relasi yang dimaksud.
- Kualitas dan kecukupan provenance.
- Apakah scope dan pengecualian diterapkan konsisten.
- Apakah model masuk akal bagi domain experts dan pemilik informasi.

Expert review tetap perlu kriteria dan catatan keputusan. “Menurut saya sudah benar” bukan audit trail.

### 3.2 Rule-based and structural validation

Untuk model berbasis RDF, SHACL dapat menyatakan constraint pada node dan property, lalu memvalidasi data graph terhadap shapes graph. Contohnya mencakup batas jumlah nilai, jenis node, datatype, class, pola nilai, dan kombinasi kondisi. SHACL menghasilkan validation report yang menunjukkan hasil pemeriksaan terhadap constraint yang ditentukan. [1]

Contoh konseptual:
- Setiap entity record harus memiliki identifier internal.
- Setiap relationship assertion harus memiliki predicate.
- Setiap assertion berstatus approved harus memiliki provenance record.
- Nilai atribut tertentu harus menggunakan controlled vocabulary.
- Relasi tertentu hanya boleh menghubungkan tipe entitas yang telah ditetapkan.

Contoh tersebut adalah aturan proyek yang perlu didefinisikan. Jangan menganggap SHACL menentukan sendiri aturan bisnis atau makna domain.

### 3.3 Ontology reasoning and consistency checks

Jika model menggunakan OWL, reasoner dapat membantu memeriksa konsekuensi logis dan inkonsistensi berdasarkan aksioma serta semantik OWL yang digunakan. OWL 2 memiliki semantik formal; hasil reasoning bergantung pada aksioma dan asumsi yang dimasukkan ke ontology. [4]

Batas penting:
- Konsistensi logis bukan bukti empiris bahwa fakta benar.
- Reasoner hanya dapat menemukan konsekuensi yang mengikuti dari representasi dan semantik yang digunakan.
- Kesalahan pemodelan atau asumsi yang tidak dimasukkan tidak akan otomatis ditemukan.
- Model yang konsisten tetap bisa tidak lengkap, tidak relevan, atau buruk untuk kebutuhan pengguna.

### 3.4 Competency questions and scenario testing

Gunakan pertanyaan dan skenario representatif untuk memeriksa apakah model dapat mendukung tugas atau pertanyaan yang menjadi tujuan model.

Contoh:
- Dapatkah model membedakan dua komponen yang memiliki nama serupa?
- Dapatkah pengguna menemukan relasi prerequisite yang memang penting?
- Dapatkah reviewer melacak sumber assertion yang disengketakan?
- Dapatkah perubahan atribut mengidentifikasi halaman dan record yang terdampak?

Metode ini memeriksa kegunaan dan kesesuaian model terhadap kebutuhan yang dinyatakan. Jangan mengulang seluruh topical coverage scoring di sini; hasil assessment 09.8 menjadi salah satu input.

### 3.5 Website architecture review

Bandingkan knowledge model yang disetujui dengan sitemap konseptual, page-role matrix, taxonomy, dan architecture specification dari 09.9. Gunakan walkthrough berbasis kebutuhan informasi, bukan sekadar memastikan semua node memiliki halaman.

Pemeriksaan ini berada pada tingkat model dan architecture alignment. Audit HTML, URL status, crawlability, canonicalization, structured data implementation, dan internal-link execution tetap milik Domain 07, 10, dan 11.

## 4. Build a Validation Framework

Validation framework mengubah prinsip kualitas menjadi aturan yang dapat dijalankan dan ditinjau.

### 4.1 Define the validation contract

Untuk setiap model atau komponen, dokumentasikan:

| Field | What to define |
|---|---|
| Model identifier | Stable ID for the model or model component |
| Purpose and scope | Intended use, included concepts, and exclusions |
| Model owner | Person or role accountable for the model |
| Authoritative definitions | Canonical schema, vocabulary, and policy documents |
| Validation dimensions | Identity, relationships, provenance, scope, architecture alignment, etc. |
| Rules and acceptance criteria | Explicit conditions that can be reviewed or tested |
| Validation method | Manual review, SHACL, reasoning, scenario test, or combination |
| Severity levels | How findings are classified and escalated |
| Review frequency and triggers | Scheduled review and event-based reassessment |
| Evidence record | Sources, reports, decisions, exceptions, and timestamps |

### 4.2 Use severity based on consequence

Severity labels are a governance convention, not a universal standard. Define them in the project and connect them to actions.

| Severity | Example | Expected response |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Entity collision changes the meaning of a high-impact model component; unsupported relation is treated as an authoritative fact | Stop affected release or use; resolve before approval unless a documented emergency exception exists |
| High | Broken identity mapping, invalid core relationship, missing provenance for a high-risk assertion | Assign owner and remediation deadline; block dependent changes where appropriate |
| Medium | Inconsistent labels, incomplete metadata, non-critical taxonomy mismatch | Schedule correction and assess downstream impact |
| Low | Cosmetic documentation issue or non-impacting metadata inconsistency | Add to maintenance backlog |

A finding's severity should reflect impact, likelihood, scope, and downstream dependency—not how annoying the warning looks in a dashboard.

### 4.3 Record validation outcomes

A useful validation report includes:

- Model version and validation date.
- Scope and components inspected.
- Rules and tool versions used.
- Data snapshot or commit identifier.
- Passed checks and failed checks.
- Findings, severity, evidence, and affected components.
- Accepted exceptions and rationale.
- Reviewer and approval status.
- Required remediation and follow-up date.

A report must state what was not checked. “No issues found” is meaningless if the reviewer only checked label formatting.

## 5. Semantic Model Governance

**Semantic model governance** is the set of ownership, decision, access, documentation, and change-control practices that keep a model understandable and dependable over time.

Governance does not require a committee for every small vocabulary. A small site may need one accountable owner and a lightweight review process. A multi-team knowledge platform may need formal roles and approval gates. Scale governance to risk and complexity.

### 5.1 Define roles and decision rights

Possible roles:

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Model owner | Accountable for purpose, scope, priorities, and approval |
| Domain expert | Reviews domain meaning, facts, terminology, and exceptions |
| Model editor / curator | Maintains entities, attributes, relationships, labels, and records |
| Evidence reviewer | Checks source quality, provenance, and assertion status |
| Architecture owner | Maintains alignment between semantic model and website IA |
| Technical steward | Maintains the technical representation and validation pipeline where one exists |
| Change approver | Authorizes material changes, deprecations, and releases |

One person may hold multiple roles. The important thing is that responsibility and decision authority are explicit.

### 5.2 Establish decision policies

Document policies for:

- Creating and retiring entity records.
- Merging or splitting entity identities.
- Adding or redefining attributes and predicates.
- Changing domain/range or other formal constraints.
- Accepting inferred or externally sourced assertions.
- Handling disputed or contradictory evidence.
- Adding external vocabulary dependencies.
- Changing preferred labels and multilingual terms.
- Deprecating concepts, relationships, or page roles.
- Approving exceptions and emergency changes.

Policies should specify who proposes, who reviews, who approves, and what evidence is required. Do not let an informal edit silently redefine a core concept.

### 5.3 Maintain provenance and decision history

Keep a traceable record of:
- Source evidence and retrieval date.
- Assertion creator or importing process.
- Reviewer and approval decision.
- Rationale for accepting or rejecting a change.
- Previous and new values.
- Related validation findings.
- Affected models, pages, and downstream consumers.

PROV-O can represent provenance entities, activities, and agents when interoperable provenance modeling is useful. A simpler change log may be enough for a small editorial knowledge model. The format is optional; traceability is not. [3]

## 6. Versioning and Change Management

A model changes when meaning, scope, structure, dependencies, or approved assertions change. Versioning must make those changes discoverable and explain their consequences.

### 6.1 What should trigger a new version?

Examples:
- Entity identity is merged, split, or redefined.
- A core predicate changes meaning or direction.
- A class hierarchy or controlled vocabulary changes.
- A constraint becomes stricter or more permissive.
- A source is invalidated and assertions are revised.
- Scope expands or contracts.
- An external ontology dependency changes.
- A model-to-website mapping changes materially.

Not every spelling correction needs a major model release. Define a versioning policy based on the impact and compatibility of changes.

### 6.2 A practical versioning convention

A project may adopt a semantic-style scheme such as:

- **Major:** incompatible change to meaning, identifiers, core relationships, or constraints that may break dependent models or systems.
- **Minor:** backward-compatible addition of concepts, attributes, relationships, or approved scope.
- **Patch:** correction that does not intentionally change the model's meaning or compatibility.

This is a project convention, not a W3C requirement and not a universal rule for ontology versioning.

For OWL 2 ontologies, the specification distinguishes an ontology IRI from an optional version IRI, which can identify a particular ontology version. That is a formal ontology identity/versioning mechanism; it should not be confused with the project's own release labels. [4]

### 6.3 Change request workflow

1. **Propose:** describe the requested change, reason, evidence, and urgency.
2. **Classify:** identify affected entities, attributes, relationships, vocabulary terms, constraints, and page roles.
3. **Assess impact:** inspect dependent models, assertions, content architecture, integrations, and consumers.
4. **Review evidence:** verify whether the change is supported and whether competing interpretations remain.
5. **Approve or reject:** record decision, owner, and rationale.
6. **Implement:** update the canonical model and its dependent representations.
7. **Validate:** run applicable structural, logical, provenance, scope, and architecture checks.
8. **Release:** publish the version and change summary.
9. **Monitor and reconcile:** confirm downstream owners have handled the change and close the request.

### 6.4 Deprecation is not deletion

When a concept, property, or entity is no longer recommended:
- Mark its status as deprecated or retired.
- Record the reason and effective date.
- Identify a replacement where one exists.
- Preserve the historical identifier and change trail when needed for traceability.
- Document migration guidance for dependent records and pages.
- Avoid silently reusing the old identifier for a different meaning.

OWL 2 includes ontology annotations such as prior-version and compatibility-related annotations; these support ontology-level version documentation but do not replace a complete operational migration policy. [4]

## 7. Monitor Change and Define Review Triggers

Scheduled reviews are useful, but event-based review is often more important. A model should be reassessed when the evidence or environment that supports it changes.

### 7.1 Scheduled review

Choose a review cadence based on:
- Rate of change in the subject domain.
- Consequence of incorrect information.
- Volume of new or revised assertions.
- Number of downstream models and website components.
- Available review capacity.

There is no universal monthly, quarterly, or annual schedule that fits every semantic model. Record why the chosen cadence is adequate.

### 7.2 Event-based review triggers

Trigger reassessment when:
- A primary standard, policy, or authoritative source changes.
- A key entity changes identity, ownership, status, or definition.
- A high-impact assertion is disputed or invalidated.
- A vocabulary or external ontology releases a breaking change.
- A model constraint begins producing unexpected failures.
- A website redesign changes page roles, information grouping, or content ownership.
- A new audience, product, service, market, or use case changes the model's scope.
- A dependent system reports a mapping or interpretation failure.

### 7.3 Maintenance backlog

Track findings as actionable work items:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Finding ID | Stable reference |
| Affected component | Entity, relation, vocabulary, rule, page role, or model |
| Description | What is wrong or uncertain |
| Evidence | Source, report, or observation |
| Severity and impact | Why the issue matters |
| Owner | Who is responsible |
| Decision | Fix, accept, defer, reject, or investigate |
| Due/review date | When it must be revisited |
| Resolution | What changed and how it was validated |

A backlog is not a dumping ground. Each item needs an owner, a decision, and a path to closure.

## 8. Align Model Changes with Website Architecture

A change in the semantic model can affect the website, but the impact is not always one-to-one. A new concept may belong inside an existing page; a changed distinction may require a new page role, revised grouping, or clearer labels.

Use an explicit impact map:

| Model change | Possible architecture impact | Responsible domain |
|---|---|---|
| Entity identity merge or split | Review page ownership, labels, and possible overlap | Domain 09.9, with Domain 08 for editorial assets |
| Concept definition changes | Review page scope, terminology, and explanatory requirements | Domain 09.9 and Domain 08 |
| Relationship semantics change | Review conceptual grouping and information pathways | Domain 09.9; Domain 11 decides actual links |
| Taxonomy or preferred label changes | Review category labels, filters, and navigation terminology | Domain 09.9; Domain 07/10 for implementation |
| Page role or content grouping changes | Review editorial planning and page-level requirements | Domain 08 and Domain 07 |
| URL or routing changes | Assess redirects, canonicalization, crawl/index behavior, and migration | Domain 10, with Domain 07 |
| Internal-link changes | Review link targets, anchors, and link implementation | Domain 11 |
| Organic performance change | Measure and interpret the outcome | Domain 14 |

The semantic model owner should not directly change URLs, redirects, HTML, internal links, or analytics definitions outside the relevant domain's process. Governance coordinates the change; it does not steal ownership from other domains.

## 9. Practical End-to-End Governance Workflow

1. **Register the model:** assign a stable model ID, owner, purpose, scope, and authoritative location.
2. **Baseline the model:** record the approved schema, vocabulary, entities, relationships, provenance rules, and architecture mapping.
3. **Define validation criteria:** specify checks, acceptance thresholds, severity, and exceptions.
4. **Run validation:** combine automated checks and human review appropriate to the model.
5. **Record findings:** preserve evidence, affected components, and validation limitations.
6. **Resolve or accept findings:** assign owners and document risk-based decisions.
7. **Review change requests:** evaluate semantic meaning, compatibility, provenance, and downstream impact.
8. **Update the canonical model:** make approved changes in the authoritative source.
9. **Revalidate dependent representations:** include graphs, vocabularies, architecture specifications, and affected website information structures.
10. **Release and document:** publish the version, change log, validation report, and migration notes.
11. **Review triggers and backlog:** schedule the next review and monitor event-based changes.

This workflow is deliberately tool-agnostic. It can be implemented with a spreadsheet and controlled review process, a version-controlled ontology repository, or a more formal knowledge-management platform. The control objective matters more than buying a fancy tool.

## 10. Example: Maintaining a Bicycle-Braking Knowledge Model

**Illustrative example only.** This scenario shows how validation and governance decisions can work; it is not a technical specification for bicycle components.

Assume a model contains:
- Entities for disc brakes, brake pads, rotors, calipers, and hydraulic actuation.
- Relationships describing component membership and functional interaction.
- Attributes for component type, compatibility, and maintenance status.
- Website architecture pages for braking-system overview, component reference, maintenance procedure, and troubleshooting.

A domain expert reports that one relationship between a component and a braking system is too broad. The existing predicate implies that every instance of the component belongs to every system of that type.

The governance process:
1. Opens a change request with the disputed assertion and source evidence.
2. Checks whether the problem is an entity identity error, an overgeneralized relationship, or a missing qualifier.
3. Reviews the predicate definition, affected records, and provenance.
4. Updates the relationship model only after domain review.
5. Runs structural checks on the affected records and reviews logical consequences where formal reasoning is used.
6. Checks whether component reference pages or overview explanations rely on the old statement.
7. Assigns editorial and technical follow-up to their respective owners.
8. Releases the change with a version note and validation record.

The important result is not “we updated a triple.” It is that the meaning, evidence, downstream impact, approval, and correction are all traceable.

## 11. Common Governance Failures

| Failure | Why it causes trouble | Better control |
|---|---|---|
| Treating structural conformance as factual truth | A perfectly shaped graph can still contain false assertions | Separate structural, logical, evidence, and domain review |
| No accountable model owner | Changes become inconsistent or nobody resolves disputes | Assign decision rights and escalation paths |
| Untracked edits to core definitions | Downstream records silently inherit changed meanings | Require change requests and versioned decisions |
| No provenance for important assertions | Reviewers cannot reconstruct why a statement was accepted | Preserve source, time, status, and decision history |
| Reusing deprecated identifiers | Old references may now point to a different meaning | Retire identifiers and provide explicit migration guidance |
| One validation tool for every problem | Tools only check the conditions and semantics they support | Combine rule checks, reasoning, expert review, and scenario tests |
| Treating every warning as critical | Teams ignore noisy reports or waste effort | Define severity by consequence and risk |
| Never reviewing external dependencies | Vocabulary or ontology changes can break assumptions | Track dependencies and release changes |
| Updating the model but not the website architecture | Website labels and page roles drift from approved meaning | Run impact analysis and coordinate with Domain 09.9 and downstream owners |
| Governance by committee without clear authority | Decisions stall and accountability disappears | Define who proposes, reviews, approves, and implements |

## 13. Key Takeaways

- Validation checks a model against explicit criteria; it does not certify universal truth.
- Structural conformance, logical consistency, evidence quality, scope fitness, and website-architecture alignment are different dimensions.
- SHACL can validate RDF graphs against declared constraints; OWL reasoning checks consequences under formal semantics. Neither replaces evidence review.
- Provenance and decision history make important assertions auditable.
- Governance requires clear ownership, decision rights, change policies, and a maintenance process.
- Versioning and deprecation must preserve compatibility information and historical traceability.
- Model changes require impact analysis across the website architecture and downstream domains.
- The appropriate governance process depends on model complexity and risk. More bureaucracy is not automatically more rigor.

The output is a maintainable knowledge model—not a ranking-factor checklist.
