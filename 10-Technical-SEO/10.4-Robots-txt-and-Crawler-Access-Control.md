# 10.4 — Robots.txt & Crawler Access Control

robots.txt is a plain-text file implementing the Robots Exclusion Protocol (REP). It communicates crawler access rules for matching URL paths; it is not authentication, authorization, or a reliable mechanism for keeping private resources out of public indexes.

## 1. Core concepts and mechanisms

A robots.txt file is conventionally placed at the root of a scheme-and-host origin. Its rules are interpreted by compliant crawlers; different crawlers may support different extensions or edge cases. Identify the crawler and specification before relying on a rule.

A file can contain user-agent groups and directives such as Disallow and Allow. Matching behavior, path syntax, precedence, encoding, and file-fetch failures must be understood according to REP and the crawler's documented implementation. [1]

Example (illustrative only):

```text
User-agent: *
Disallow: /private-preview/
Allow: /private-preview/public-example.html
Sitemap: https://example.com/sitemap.xml
```

robots.txt controls whether a crawler may fetch matching URLs. A robots meta directive or X-Robots-Tag is discovered in a fetched response and can communicate indexing/serving restrictions. If crawling is blocked, the crawler may be unable to see a noindex directive. [2]

Before deployment, validate syntax, group placement, allowed/disallowed URLs, staging/production separation, and access to critical rendering resources. Fetch the live file after deployment and inspect crawler reports or logs. Do not use robots.txt as a password, to conceal sensitive data, or while expecting the crawler to read a noindex directive on the blocked page.

## 2. Practical validation

Use representative URLs and templates, inspect the actual HTTP response and rendered output, document the environment and date, and retest after production deployment. A tool result is evidence about the test it performed, not a universal certification.

## 3. Limitations and ownership

Attribute search-engine-specific behavior to the engine that documents it. Distinguish technical validity from indexing selection, ranking, and search presentation. Use adjacent-domain links from the [Domain 10 index](README.md) to preserve canonical ownership.
