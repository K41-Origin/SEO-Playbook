# 12.6 — Link Spam Policies, Risk, and Remediation

**Domain:** [12 — Off-Page SEO](README.md)  
**Topic role:** Practitioner-side policy compliance, risk review, and corrective action

Link-risk management is the process of identifying external-link practices that may violate a search engine's policies, understanding the actual evidence, and taking proportionate corrective action. It is not a campaign to remove every unfamiliar or low-metric backlink.

The current Google link-spam definition and examples are covered in [03.7 — Spam Detection and Demotion Systems](../03-Search-Engine-Algorithms/03.7-Spam-Detection-and-Demotion-Systems.md). This topic focuses on applying those policy boundaries to site-owner investigation and remediation.

## 1. Understand the Policy Before Auditing

Before classifying an arrangement, review Google's current policy. Examine the actual relationship, purpose, acquisition history, and whether the link is appropriately qualified; do not infer a violation from an unfamiliar source or low third-party metric alone. [1]

An advertisement or sponsorship is not inherently prohibited. Google's documentation says paid placements should be qualified using `rel="sponsored"` (with `nofollow` still acceptable). [2]

This topic explains the site owner's response. Search-engine detection, neutralization, demotion, and broader spam systems remain the canonical subject of [03.7 — Spam Detection and Demotion Systems](../03-Search-Engine-Algorithms/03.7-Spam-Detection-and-Demotion-Systems.md).

## 2. Distinguish a Risk Indicator From a Violation

A backlink may look unusual without being a policy violation. Examples include:
- A sudden increase after genuine press coverage.
- Links from a small or unfamiliar website.
- A sitewide link that exists for navigation or attribution.
- Scraped or automatically generated references created by third parties.
- Links with generic or exact-match anchor text in otherwise legitimate editorial context.

Conversely, a polished publisher website may sell placements designed to pass ranking credit.

Do not classify links using a single metric, a domain-name list, or a sudden-change chart alone. Investigate the source page, relationship, placement, purpose, acquisition history, and whether the site owner or an agent arranged the activity.

## 3. Investigate With Traceable Evidence

A proportionate review should:

1. Define the suspected activity and time period.
2. Collect available link data from Search Console and relevant third-party tools.
3. Preserve source URLs, target URLs, anchor text, dates, provider, and export context.
4. Inspect representative referring pages and relevant acquisition records.
5. Identify whether the site owner, agency, affiliate, vendor, or another party arranged the links.
6. Compare observed practices with the current applicable spam policy.
7. Classify findings as confirmed, plausible risk, not substantiated, or requiring more evidence.
8. Record the rationale and confidence for each material conclusion.

Google's Search Console Links report is a sample, not a complete list of all backlinks; do not assume that an export contains every link. [3]

## 4. Correct the Underlying Practice First

If the site or its agents created manipulative links, stop the activity and correct the source of the problem.

Depending on the circumstances, corrective actions may include:
- Ending paid-link or automated-link arrangements intended to manipulate rankings.
- Asking publishers to remove links that were created in violation of policy.
- Asking for appropriate qualification of paid or sponsored links.
- Correcting contracts or vendor requirements that mandate unqualified links.
- Reviewing agency and supplier practices.
- Preserving evidence of remediation and ownership.

Google's manual-action guidance recommends reviewing the unnatural-link pattern, contacting site owners to remove or qualify violating links, and documenting remediation. [4]

## 5. Manual Actions and Reconsideration

A manual action is a human-applied measure taken after a reviewer determines that pages are not compliant with Google's spam policies. The Manual Actions report in Search Console identifies whether a site has a manual action and describes affected patterns. [4]

For an unnatural-links action, a site owner should:
1. Review the specific issue in Search Console.
2. Identify the link practices that caused the problem.
3. Remove or appropriately qualify links where possible.
4. Preserve evidence of good-faith remediation.
5. Use the disavow process only where its documented conditions apply.
6. Submit a reconsideration request with a clear account of the corrective work.

A reconsideration request is not a guarantee that a manual action will be revoked. The review outcome depends on whether the issue has been adequately addressed.

## 6. Disavow Is an Advanced, Narrow Tool

Google says most sites do not need the disavow tool because its systems can generally assess which links to trust. Google recommends considering disavow only when both conditions apply:

- There is a considerable number of spammy, artificial, or low-quality links pointing to the site; and
- Those links have caused, or are likely to cause, a manual action.

Google also warns that incorrect use can harm search performance. Its guidance recommends trying to remove problematic links first where possible. [5]

Therefore:
- Do not disavow every unfamiliar, low-DR, or low-DA domain.
- Do not use disavow as routine backlink hygiene.
- Do not assume that a third-party toxicity score establishes Google's view.
- Preserve the evidence and rationale behind each disavow decision.
- Recheck Google's current documentation before using the tool because its interface and eligibility details may change.

## 7. Risk Governance

A durable link-risk programme should maintain:
- A record of link acquisition suppliers and contractual terms.
- Disclosure and qualification standards for commercial placements.
- Approval requirements for campaigns with external-link objectives.
- A process for investigating unusual link patterns.
- Evidence retention for removals, qualifications, and reconsideration requests.
- A named owner for policy review and remediation.
- A periodic review triggered by material changes, incidents, or platform-policy updates.

Do not outsource accountability to an SEO vendor. The site owner should understand what activity was performed on its behalf.

## Scope Boundaries

This topic owns practitioner-side interpretation, compliance, risk triage, and remediation. It does not reproduce search-engine spam-detection algorithms or system behavior (Domain 03.7), general backlink analysis methodology (12.5), or broad technical site remediation (Domain 10).
