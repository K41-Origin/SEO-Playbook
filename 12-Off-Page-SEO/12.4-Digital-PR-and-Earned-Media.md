# 12.4 — Digital PR and Earned Media

**Domain:** [12 — Off-Page SEO](README.md)  
**Topic role:** Campaign-based external visibility, media relations, and editorial coverage

Digital public relations applies public-relations principles and practices in digital environments. It can support off-page SEO when research, expertise, stories, or useful resources attract relevant online coverage and editorial references. But digital PR is not simply link building with a press-release template. Its broader purpose is to build understanding, relationships, and reputation with relevant publics.

The Chartered Institute of Public Relations describes public relations as a discipline concerned with reputation, understanding, support, and relationships between an organization and its publics. [1] That framing matters: a campaign can succeed without producing a backlink, and a backlink alone does not prove that a campaign succeeded.

## 1. Digital PR, Earned Media, and SEO

- **Public relations (PR):** planned communication and relationship work involving an organization and its relevant publics.
- **Digital PR:** PR activity conducted through or materially involving digital channels, publishers, creators, communities, and online audiences.
- **Earned media:** coverage or attention that a publisher or other party chooses to provide rather than a placement purchased as advertising.
- **Editorial link:** a hyperlink included as part of the publisher's editorial treatment.
- **Digital PR for SEO:** a practical application in which relevant coverage and references may also create useful external links or referral paths.

These categories can overlap but are not synonymous. Earned coverage may contain no link. A paid placement is not earned media merely because it appears on a publisher's website. A campaign may generate a link without generating meaningful audience engagement.

## 2. What Makes a Story Worth Covering?

A campaign needs a defensible reason for an independent publisher to care. Potential story foundations include:

- Original research with transparent methods and relevant findings.
- New data that answers a question important to a defined audience.
- Expert analysis that adds context to a current issue.
- A useful public resource or tool.
- A documented case study with clear evidence and limitations.
- A meaningful organizational initiative or development.

A headline is not a substitute for evidence. Data-led campaigns should disclose the data source, sample, time period, definitions, methodology, limitations, and any material conflicts of interest. Avoid presenting a weak correlation or small convenience sample as a universal finding.

Research design and content production belong to [Domain 08 — Content SEO](../08-Content-SEO/README.md). This topic owns the communication and earned-media application of those assets.

## 3. Plan Around Audiences and Relationships

A practical campaign begins by defining:

1. **Organizational objective:** what meaningful change or relationship is sought?
2. **Communication objective:** what should a particular audience know, understand, feel, or do?
3. **Target publics:** which journalists, publishers, professional groups, customers, researchers, or stakeholders matter?
4. **Evidence and story:** what can be stated accurately and supported?
5. **Channels and activity:** which media relations, events, briefings, digital assets, or community routes fit?
6. **Risks:** what could be misunderstood, challenged, or misrepresented?
7. **Evaluation:** how will outputs, audience responses, outcomes, and organizational impact be distinguished?

The 2025 Certificate in Principles of Public Relations Study Guide published by the Universal Accreditation Board distinguishes inputs, activities, outputs, out-takes, outcomes, and impacts. It emphasizes measurable objectives, appropriate quantitative and qualitative measures, and transparent evaluation. This is useful because a count of published articles is not evidence by itself of improved understanding, trust, or organizational results. [2]

## 4. Media Relations and Campaign Execution

A responsible workflow may include:

- Researching publications and journalists whose coverage genuinely relates to the subject.
- Preparing a concise evidence-led story brief.
- Providing source documents, data, methodology, visuals, and access to relevant experts.
- Offering clear contact details and responding accurately to questions.
- Respecting editorial deadlines, independence, and decisions.
- Correcting factual errors through appropriate channels.
- Recording the published coverage and its actual context.

A pitch should explain why the story matters to that publication's audience. It should not assume the journalist owes coverage or a link. Do not offer undisclosed incentives for positive coverage or require a particular anchor text.

## 5. Earned, Paid, Shared, and Owned Are Different

A campaign may use several media categories:

| Category | Typical example | Main distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Earned | Independent editorial coverage | Publisher or stakeholder chooses to cover the story |
| Paid | Sponsored article, advertising, paid creator placement | Placement is purchased or materially compensated |
| Shared | Community or social distribution | Content is circulated through social participation |
| Owned | Organization's website, newsletter, or publication | The organization controls the publishing property |

A single campaign may use all four. Do not report a paid placement as earned coverage. Where links are paid or sponsored, follow relevant link-qualification guidance such as Google's `rel="sponsored"` recommendation. [4]

## 6. Measure PR Beyond Backlinks

A balanced evaluation separates:

- **Outputs:** what was produced or published—briefings, placements, coverage, events, assets.
- **Out-takes:** audience attention, recall, understanding, and engagement.
- **Outcomes:** changes in attitudes, trust, preference, intention, or behavior.
- **Impact:** contribution to organizational objectives, such as stronger relationships, reputation, qualified demand, or other defined outcomes.

This model avoids the common mistake of treating potential reach, article count, or backlinks as proof of business impact. The UAB/PRSA study guide points readers to AMEC's Integrated Evaluation Framework as a practical structure for connecting objectives, benchmarks, and measures across these stages. [2]

SEO-specific outcomes should be reported separately: earned editorial links, referring pages, referral visits, and observed organic-search changes are different measures. Attribution requires care because many other factors can affect search performance.

## 7. Risks and Editorial Integrity

- Do not fabricate research, sample sizes, quotations, experts, or news hooks.
- Do not disguise paid coverage as independent editorial work.
- Do not require a link or keyword-rich anchor as a condition of coverage.
- Do not distribute low-value press releases at scale primarily to create links.
- Do not claim that a campaign improved rankings without suitable evidence.
- Correct material inaccuracies rather than treating coverage volume as the only objective.

Google explicitly identifies certain paid advertorial links, optimized guest-post links, and press-release links intended to pass ranking credit as examples of link spam. Properly qualified advertising links are treated differently. [3]
